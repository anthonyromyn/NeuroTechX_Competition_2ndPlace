
# EEG Brain Age Prediction — 2nd Place, NeuroTechX 2022

**Deep Learning · Ensemble Modeling · Feature Engineering · Tabular Modeling · Automated Machine Learning**

## Overview

This repository documents the machine learning solution developed for State++'s entry in the 2022 NeuroTechX Global Hackathon Brain Age Prediction Competition.

Our team placed **2nd among 150+ international teams**, achieving a mean absolute error (MAE) of **1.60 years** on the competition's held-out test set.

As head of predictive modeling at State++, I co-developed the modeling strategy, theoretical approach, and model-selection decisions with other team members and was solely responsible for implementation of the predictive modeling pipeline, including feature extraction, model training, experimentation, ensembling, and final prediction generation.

The challenge was to predict chronological age from resting-state EEG recordings, extracting reliable predictive information from noisy, high-dimensional neural time-series data.

## Modeling Pipeline

The solution combined complementary representations of EEG activity, including features extracted from neural time series and predictions generated by trained models.

These representations were integrated through an Auto-PyTorch regression workflow to produce the final age predictions.

## Technical Background & Methodology

The modeling approach drew on established research in EEG signal processing, brain-age prediction, and deep learning.

### Deep Learning

The convolutional neural network was based on the Deep4Net architecture introduced by Schirrmeister et al. (2017), with additional methodological foundations from Engemann et al. (2022) and Banville et al. (2022).

The architecture was adapted for EEG age regression, allowing the model to learn predictive representations directly from neural time-series data.

### Engineered EEG Features

Three categories of features were investigated:

- **Handcrafted features:** Statistical, temporal, and spectral descriptors of EEG activity, drawing on approaches described by Engemann et al. (2022).
- **Filterbank-Riemann features:** Covariance-based representations across frequency bands, using Riemannian geometry to capture relationships between EEG channels.
- **Spectral parameterization (FOOOF):** Periodic and aperiodic components of EEG power spectra, including frequency-resolved features between 1–40 Hz, based on Donoghue et al. (2020).

These methods provided complementary representations of neural activity beyond those learned directly by the convolutional network.

### Model Combination

Regularized ridge regression was used with engineered EEG representations, while the convolutional neural network provided predictions learned directly from EEG time-series data.

The final modeling stage supplied engineered features and base-model predictions to Auto-PyTorch for automated regression modeling.

The objective was to combine complementary predictive information from multiple modeling approaches rather than relying exclusively on a single model family.

## Reproducibility & Limitations

### Historical Research Environment

The original analyses were developed in Google Colab using Python, PyTorch, Braindecode, MNE, scikit-learn, Auto-PyTorch, and other scientific Python libraries.

The notebooks depend on the original competition data, saved intermediate features, model outputs, and Google Drive directory paths.

These data and intermediate files are not included in the repository. Some software versions and dependencies may also require a historical environment to reproduce the original analyses.

Consequently, the notebooks document the original modeling implementation but are not currently packaged as a fully reproducible end-to-end pipeline.
